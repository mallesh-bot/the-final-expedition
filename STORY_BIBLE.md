# THE FINAL EXPEDITION — Story Bible

> **Status: LOCKED (v1.0).** All dialogue, collectibles, names, locations and events in the game must be consistent with this document. Changes require an entry in the Revision Log at the bottom with a reason.

---

## 1. Logline

A former mountain-rescue climber follows a letter her grandmother could not have written — dated three years after she "died" on a doomed 1958 expedition — into a hidden cloud-forest gorge, and discovers the expedition didn't vanish by accident. It chose to disappear, to protect the valley below from the very thing it came to find.

## 2. Tone

Mysterious, adventurous, grounded. Human-scale heroism: people are brave, frightened, funny and wrong. Danger comes from rock, water, gravity, and other people's choices — never from magic. The Ysharu were engineers and astronomers, not sorcerers. Humour is dry and character-driven, never undercutting a genuine emotional beat.

## 3. Characters

### Ines Kovač — protagonist
- **Age:** 36. **Born:** 1990, Rijeka, Croatia; raised in London.
- **Look:** Lean, climber's shoulders, sun-weathered olive skin, dark auburn hair tied in a low knot, a thin pale scar through her left eyebrow. Olive-drab field jacket with rolled sleeves over a rust-orange base layer, climbing harness, grey-brown cargo trousers, worn leather boots, canvas satchel. Carries her grandmother's unsent letter in a waxed envelope inside her jacket.
- **Background:** Eleven years with an alpine mountain-rescue team. Left after a recovery she couldn't make in time (a subject she does not discuss). Now works as a freelance archival researcher cataloguing estates — which is how she found the letter.
- **Personality:** Dry, stubborn, careful, observant. Better with rock than with people. Reads terrain the way other people read faces. Deflects feelings with precise, understated jokes. Never takes a risk she hasn't measured — until this expedition asks her to.
- **Motivation:** The truth about Marta. Not treasure, not fame. Why did a woman declared dead in 1958 write to her infant daughter in 1961 — and why did that daughter (Ines's mother, Lena) never get to read it?
- **Arc:** From someone who needs to *know* to someone who can *keep* a secret for the right reason. At the end she chooses to reseal what Marta sealed.

### Tobias "Toby" Mensah-Reyes — second lead
- **Age:** 38. Epigrapher (ancient scripts). Ghanaian-Filipino, raised in Leeds.
- **Look:** Tall, round spectacles taped at one hinge, short twists under a sun-bleached bucket hat, linen shirt, too many pens, a field notebook held together with rubber bands.
- **Background:** Ines's friend from university (they shared a disastrous first-year caving club trip). Brilliant, underfunded, and one failed grant away from leaving academia. He has spent nine years on the partially-deciphered Ysharu script.
- **Personality:** Warm, anxious, funny, talks when nervous (constantly). Afraid of heights, which he mentions whenever there is height. Genuinely thrilled by every carved line.
- **Motivation:** Publish a complete Ysharu decipherment. The Hollow Meridian's inscriptions would make his career — and prove he wasn't wasting his life.
- **Secret (revealed Ch.4):** To afford the trip, Toby accepted money from Corwin Aldercott's company in exchange for sharing their route. He told himself Corwin only wanted the story.
- **Arc:** From telling himself small lies for good reasons to telling the truth when it costs him everything he wanted. In the end he publishes the script — but not the location.

### Corwin Aldercott — antagonist
- **Age:** 52. Mining and hydrological engineer; head of Aldercott Survey Ltd., a failing consultancy.
- **Look:** Silver at the temples, neat beard, expensive expedition gear worn like a suit. Carries his grandfather's brass pocket compass.
- **Background:** Grandson of Sir Edmund Aldercott, financier of the 1958 expedition. After Edmund returned alone claiming an avalanche killed everyone, the victims' families sued; the Aldercott name became a byword for reckless vanity and the family fortune evaporated. Corwin grew up in the wreckage. He found Edmund's private diaries (hidden in a sealed estate box) which prove the expedition *reached* the Hollow Meridian.
- **Motivation (understandable):** Vindication. If he can prove the expedition succeeded — that it found the greatest archaeological site of the century — then his grandfather wasn't a fool who got eight people killed for nothing. He also has investors, debts, and a crew he's promised to pay.
- **Flaw:** He believes Edmund's version of events and treats safety, consent, and other people's warnings as obstacles to be engineered around. He uses explosives in a structure he doesn't understand.
- **Arc:** In the sanctum he learns Edmund lied — that Edmund tried to take the Lens knowing it would drown the valley, and the others stopped him. Corwin chooses the truth over the name. He helps reseal the Sunlock and, afterwards, publicly clears the families his grandfather slandered.

### Dace Holloway — Corwin's crew foreman (supporting, Ch.4–5)
- Ex-military demolitions contractor. Pragmatic, paid by the job. Loyal to the money, not to Corwin. When Corwin chooses to reseal the Sunlock, Dace tries to take the Lens anyway. He is the physical threat of the final chapter; Corwin is the moral one.

### Marta Kovač — the absent heart of the story
- **Born:** 1929, Rijeka. Photographer, 1958 Aldercott Expedition.
- Left her three-year-old daughter Lena with her sister Vesna in London to join the expedition — the job would pay for a home.
- Officially died in the "avalanche" of June 1958. Actually survived, sealed the Hollow Meridian with Dr. Varne, and lived the rest of her life in the highland village of Tamsa Alta as a keeper of the secret.
- Her voice appears through journal pages, photographs, scratched initials, and finally a reel-to-reel recording in the sanctum antechamber (Ch.5).
- She speaks in precise, visual sentences — a photographer's eye. Signs notes "— M."

### Dr. Hollis Varne — 1958 expedition leader
- Geologist. Methodical, principled. First to understand what the Sunlock does. Chooses to fake the expedition's death rather than let Edmund return with the Lens. Died in Tamsa Alta in 1971. Appears only in documents and Marta's recording.

### Sir Edmund Aldercott — 1958 financier
- Charismatic, impatient, sure the find would make him immortal. Tried to remove the Lens. Stopped by Varne and Marta, he fled; the route collapsed behind him (Varne's charges). Found by a search party, he claimed an avalanche and that all others died. Spent the rest of his life unable to find the way back. Died 1989.

### Minor 1958 members (names appear on crates, journal pages, and the camp roster board)
- **Anselm Roy** — surveyor (the theodolite in Camp Four is his). Survived; lived in Tamsa Alta.
- **Pilar Estévez** — expedition medic. Survived; later left the valley under a new name.
- **Teodor Anca** — local guide from Tamsa Alta; the reason the survivors were taken in.
- **Walter Bright, Ivo Sand, Hugh Mercer** — porters/assistants hired in the lowland town; left Camp Four before the ridge ("the porters will not go past the red rock"). They were never in danger, and Edmund's report that "all perished" was the first lie.

## 4. Relationships

- **Ines ↔ Toby:** Old, easy friendship built on mutual teasing. Toby is the only person Ines has told about the letter. His betrayal in Ch.4 wounds her precisely because she trusted him with it. Reconciliation is earned in Ch.5 when he risks the fall he's terrified of to help her.
- **Ines ↔ Marta:** A grandmother she never met; a voice she only knows from a single letter. Ines is looking for Marta and slowly realises she is *becoming* her — someone who keeps a promise nobody else will ever know about.
- **Ines ↔ Corwin:** Mirrors. Both are grandchildren trying to settle a debt to someone they never really knew. Ines wants the truth; Corwin wants a particular truth. Their conflict is about what you owe the dead versus the living.
- **Toby ↔ Corwin:** Transactional. Corwin flatters Toby's scholarship; Toby wants to believe him.
- **Corwin ↔ Dace:** Employer and contractor. Their alliance breaks the moment Corwin stops paying in the currency Dace cares about.

## 5. World

### The Cordillera Velada
A fictional range of cloud-forest mountains: sheer limestone gorges, waterfalls, perpetual mist, and old-growth forest so dense the valleys are effectively unmapped. The lowland river town of **San Rocín** is the last road. Upstream, the **Sael River** runs through a series of hanging valleys to its source in the glacial **Lake Iruvai**, held high in a basin behind the ridges.

### The Ysharu (fictional civilisation)
- Flourished c. 900–1450 CE in the high gorges. Astronomers and hydraulic engineers. They carved cities *into* gorges, using sunlight entering through precisely cut apertures as calendars, and managed water with sluices, cisterns and spillways.
- **Belief:** The sun is **the Watcher**, who walks across the sky through three houses — **the Rising House**, **the Open Eye** (zenith), and **the Setting House**. To read, you face away from the valley (toward the mountain), so the sun rises at your right hand; Ysharu script therefore runs **right to left**, the way the Watcher walks.
- **Glyphs used in gameplay:** *Rising* (half-disc with rays below a line), *Open Eye* (an almond eye with a dot), *Setting* (half-disc with rays above a line, inverted), *Serpent* (a wavy river-line — the sign for water; a common decoy in their locks, meaning "not the sky").
- **The Year of the Drowned Sky (c. 1440):** A glacial-lake outburst from Lake Iruvai destroyed the lower Ysharu cities. The survivors built the **Sunlock**, a regulating sluice-and-counterweight mechanism in the heart of their last city, which drains Lake Iruvai slowly and safely through the mountain. Its keystone is the **Meridian Lens**. They then sealed the city, left, and dispersed into the highland villages. Tamsa Alta's families descend from them.

### The Hollow Meridian
The last Ysharu city, carved into the walls of a deep gorge behind the ridge above Camp Four. Terraces, aqueducts and sun-shafts spiral down to the **Sanctum of the Sunlock** at the gorge floor.

### The Meridian Lens (the artifact)
A disc of clear rock crystal set in a bronze astrolabe-ring, roughly 40 cm across, with **nine facets** around its rim. Placed in the Sunlock, it focuses the noon sun onto a sequence of counterweight triggers that keep the sluices regulated. Removed, the sluices slowly fail; within months Lake Iruvai would breach, flooding the Sael valley — including San Rocín (≈ 40,000 people today). **One of the nine facets is missing** (see Unresolved Mysteries).

## 7. The Central Mystery — and its reveals (in order)

1. **Ch.1:** The camp was abandoned in a hurry, but not destroyed. The crates were chalked "DO NOT SHIP" — someone wanted the find kept secret. The ribbon route markers up the cliff are *newer than 1958*: someone has been maintaining them.
2. **Ch.2:** Marta's photographs (developed plates found in a cache) show the team alive and working inside the gorge **after** the date Edmund said the avalanche happened.
3. **Ch.3:** Toby deciphers the inner wall: the Ysharu sealed the city on purpose, "so the sky would not drown the valley again". The Lens is not treasure; it is a keystone.
4. **Ch.4:** Corwin's crew arrives with Edmund's diaries. Toby's deal with Corwin is revealed. Corwin's blasting to "open" the sanctum destabilises the gorge.
5. **Ch.5:** In the sanctum antechamber, Marta's 1979 recording tells the truth: Edmund tried to take the Lens knowing the consequences; the survivors faked their deaths to make sure nobody came back. Marta addresses Lena directly — and, unknowingly, Ines.

## 8. Chapter Summaries

### Chapter 1 — THE DISCOVERY
**Location:** The lower Sael valley at dusk after rain: the river landing, the jungle trail, the abandoned 1958 **Camp Four**, the red-rock cliff with its waterfall, and the ridge-top **Gate of the Watching Sun**.
**Beats:**
- Opening: the camera descends the misted valley. Toby stays with the boat at the river landing (heights; "heroically guarding the boat"). Ines goes ahead with a radio.
- The trail has been cut — not recently, but long after 1958.
- Camp Four: tents collapsed, dinner still set on the stove, crates stencilled ALDERCOTT 1958 and chalked DO NOT SHIP. Anselm Roy's theodolite still points up the ridge. Ines finds three of **Marta's journal pages** and a **Ysharu Sun Token**.
- The red-rock cliff: a route marked with red cloth ribbons and rusted pitons. Ines notices the knots are new.
- On the ridge: the Gate of the Watching Sun — three carved stone drums before a sealed door, flanked by murals of the Watcher's path. Marta's initials "M.K." are scratched into the gate's base.
- **Puzzle:** Using Marta's sketch ("stand with your back to the valley and it rises at your right hand"), the murals, and the real setting sun on her left, Ines sets the drums right-to-left: Rising, Open Eye, Setting.
- **Cinematic:** The gate grinds open in a storm of dust, revealing the misted gorge of the Hollow Meridian and a waterfall plunging into a carved city. "Toby… there's a whole city down there."

### Chapter 2 — INTO THE UNKNOWN
**Location:** The upper terraces of the Hollow Meridian: waterfall-fed aqueducts, overgrown ruined terraces, rope bridges from 1958.
**Beats:** Traversal descent along the gorge wall; Ines finds Marta's cache of developed photographic plates proving the team was alive after the "avalanche". Toby, now following at a distance via the rope route, copies inscriptions. Set piece: an aqueduct span gives way under the waterfall's force. Ends at the mouth of the underground sun-shafts.

### Chapter 3 — THE HIDDEN PATH
**Location:** The underground sun-shafts and mirror chambers beneath the terraces.
**Beats:** Light-redirection puzzles using polished bronze mirrors and the Ysharu sky-houses. Toby deciphers the inner wall: the city was sealed "so the sky would not drown the valley again". First sign of other people: fresh boot prints and a drilled blast-hole.

### Chapter 4 — THE EXPEDITION
**Location:** The central gorge and the great bridge above the sanctum.
**Beats:** Corwin's crew, guided by Toby's shared route, arrives with Edmund's diaries. Toby's deal is revealed; Ines walks away from him. Corwin argues his case; Ines warns him. Dace blasts the sanctum's upper seal. The gorge begins to collapse: a major cinematic traversal escape across the breaking bridge and falling terraces. Ines is separated from everyone and descends alone.

### Chapter 5 — THE FINAL EXPEDITION
**Location:** The Sanctum of the Sunlock at the gorge floor; Marta's antechamber.
**Beats:** Ines finds Marta's reel-to-reel recorder and plays the 1979 message. Corwin arrives, hears the end of it, and breaks. Dace tries to take the Lens; the Sunlock begins to fail. Final puzzle-under-pressure: re-seating the Lens and realigning the sun-triggers as noon passes, with Toby (terrified, roped) and Corwin helping. The Sunlock settles. The group leaves through the flooded lower passage Marta described. **Ending:** Weeks later in Tamsa Alta, an elderly woman gives Ines a photograph Marta took of the sealed gate in 1979, and on the back, in the same handwriting as the letter: *"For whoever comes next. Keep it closed. — M."* Ines ties a fresh red ribbon at the foot of the cliff trail.

## 10. Ending

Ines reseals the Sunlock. The Hollow Meridian remains hidden. Toby publishes the decipherment of Ysharu script from his notebooks, with the location withheld — at the cost of the career-making "discovery" paper he wanted. Corwin publishes Edmund's diaries and a statement clearing the 1958 families. Ines reads Marta's letter aloud at her mother's grave. Final image: a fresh red ribbon on a branch at the foot of the cliff trail, knotted in the same distinctive way as the ones she found.

## 11. Unresolved Mysteries (deliberately left open)

- **The missing ninth facet** of the Meridian Lens — the Sunlock works without it. What did it do, and who has it?
- **Who has been maintaining the ribbons** on the cliff route for sixty years? (Tamsa Alta families? Marta herself, until when?)
- **What happened to Pilar Estévez** after she left the valley under a new name?
- The murals hint at *other* sealed cities further up the range.

## 12. Dialogue & Writing Style Guide

- Short lines. People interrupt, deflect, understate.
- Ines: dry, precise, few words. Never speechifies. Emotion leaks through restraint.
- Toby: talks too much when scared, uses scholarly words then undercuts them. Loves every inscription.
- Corwin: measured, persuasive, sounds reasonable; his menace is certainty.
- Marta (text/recording): visual, observant, signs "— M."
- No modern-internet slang. No quips during genuinely tragic moments.
- Environmental storytelling first; exposition only through documents and natural conversation.

## 13. Canon Text Used in Chapter 1 (locked)

**Journal page 1 — "Camp Four"**
> 14 May 1958. Camp Four. Hollis says three more days to the ridge. Edmund counts the crates every evening as if they were already full. The porters will not go past the red rock, and I don't blame them — the mist here has weight. — M.

**Journal page 2 — "The Watching Sun"** (includes a sketch of three drums)
> The gate reads as the sun walks. Stand with your back to the valley and it rises at your right hand, climbs to the Watcher's open eye, and sets at your left. Three drums, three houses. Hollis says the water-sign is a lie told to strangers. I drew what I could before the light went. — M.

**Journal page 3 — "Do Not Ship"**
> 2 June. Edmund chalked every crate DO NOT SHIP after Hollis read the inner wall. Nothing is to be recorded until he has "secured the find". I am photographing everything anyway. Lena, if you ever read this — your mother was stubborn for a reason. — M.

**Relic — "Ysharu Sun Token"**
> A bronze disc stamped with a single open eye. Nine notches around the rim; one has been filed smooth.
